A SMALL WINDOW INTO AN EXTRA DIRECTION

Meet the fourth dimension.

A square has 4 corners. A cube has 8. A tesseract has 16—and one more direction than we can point to. Turn it, project it, and see what that extra direction does.

The extra direction

We call it w. Rotating between x and w changes the 3D projection, even though the tesseract stays rigid in 4D.

Why a cube inside a cube?

At some angles, 4D perspective makes one set of corners look smaller. The connecting edges run along the fourth direction. Neither cube is physically inside the other in 4D.

A projection, not a slice

This view projects every corner. A slice would show only where the shape intersects a lower-dimensional space—a different experiment.

What are we actually seeing?

The tesseract is projected into 3D, then drawn on this 2D screen. Dragging rotates that 3D view. The slider rotates the original 4D geometry.
